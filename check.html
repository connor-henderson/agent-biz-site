Where the benchmarks come from

  • Sold multiples: 855 laundromat sales on BizBuySell, 2021–2025: median 3.50× seller's discretionary earnings (SDE), middle half 2.72×–4.50×; the 2025 average alone was 4.12×. Aging machines or a short lease pull multiples toward 3×. Revenue multiple: 5-year median 1.21×, and a valuation firm's range of 1.19×–1.78×. Among those sales, median SDE ($76,560) was 34.8% of median revenue ($219,878), a ratio of medians. All as reproduced by Iconic, July 2026 (iconic.co). Self-reported, national, all store sizes.
  • Utilities, turns per day, and vend prices: American Coin-Op 2026 reader survey. Utilities averaged 19.5% of revenue in 2025. Turns averaged 5.1 for front loaders and 3.8 for top loaders. The most common prices were $3.00 top load, $3.50 for 20 lb, $5.00 for 30 lb, and $6.00 for 40 lb; the $4.50 default above is our blend of those. It's an unscientific poll (americancoinop.com).
  • Dryer income and turns range: dryers bring in 40–60% of washer income, and washers generally run three to eight or more turns a day, per the Coin Laundry Association (laundryassociation.org). This tool uses 50%.
  • Loan term: SBA business-acquisition loans run up to 10 years under the current rules (SOP 50 10 8.1), per a lender-law firm's summary (Starfield & Smith). Under an earlier version of SBA's rules (SOP 50 10 7.1), the lease including borrower-only options should last at least as long as the loan when equipment attached to the space is a substantial part of the collateral, ideally with extra time as a buffer (summary of the prior rule). We haven't confirmed the current wording.
  • Our own rule: earnings above 45% of revenue need line-item proof before you rely on them.

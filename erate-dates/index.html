The 28-day rule, in USAC's words

  • The Federal Communications Commission's (FCC) rules require applicants to wait at least 28 days after their FCC Form 470 is certified in the E-Rate Productivity Center (EPC) before selecting a service provider, executing any contracts for contracted services, or signing and submitting an FCC Form 471. (28-Day Waiting Period)
  • The waiting period starts the calendar day (including weekends and holidays) you certify your FCC Form 470. The day you certify the form is day one. For example, if you certify on 1/1/2024, your Allowable Contract Date is 1/29/2024.
  • You cannot certify the FCC Form 471 before the Allowable Vendor Selection/Contract Date (ACD), which is 28 days after your FCC Form 470 is submitted and certified in the EPC. (FCC Form 471 Filing)
  • Applicants may set a later bid deadline by including it in their FCC Form 470 narrative or their request for proposals (RFP). This deadline must be at least 28 days after certifying their FCC Form 470 or longer depending on your state or local procurement rules or regulations. Beginning in Funding Year 2025, if applicants wish to make any substantive corrections, they can attach a new RFP document to their certified FCC Form 470, and the new 28-day waiting period will be recalculated automatically in EPC. (28-Day Waiting Period)
  • FCC rules require that applicants carefully consider all bids received before they start their bid evaluation process, even if the minimum 28-day waiting period has lapsed. (28-Day Waiting Period)
  • The minimum 28-day waiting period is tracked automatically in EPC. This is the allowable contract date (ACD) found on your FCC Form 470 Receipt Notification Letter (RNL) sent to you when you certified your form. (E-Rate News Brief (October 2026))

So the count is plain calendar days. Certify on a Friday and the allowable contract date is the Friday four weeks later; a weekend or a holiday doesn't move it. USAC's own data agrees: in its Form 470 dataset (jp7a-89nd, as updated November 6, 2026), 24,500 of the 25,069 certified FY2026 and FY2027 forms (97.7%) carry an allowable contract date exactly 28 days after certification, among them 663 that fall on a Saturday, 558 on a Sunday, 124 on Thanksgiving Day 2025, 6 on Christmas Day 2025 and 149 on New Year's Day 2026. None is earlier. The other 569 are later; the data doesn't say why, though attaching a new RFP to a certified form restarts the count in EPC (above). USAC's column note: Earliest date on which an applicant can sign a contract for contracted services or enter into an arrangement for tariffed (T) or month to-month (MTM) services with a service provider is 28 days after the certified date.

The day is the Eastern Time day. USAC's column note for the certification time: Exact date and time the FCC Form 470 was certified (EST). In the same data, 107 of the 111 forms certified between midnight and 3 a.m. Eastern carry an allowable contract date 28 days after that Eastern date, and none 27 days. So a Form 470 certified at 10 p.m. Pacific counts from the next day, the calculator starts on today's Eastern date, and the last day to certify ends at midnight Eastern.

The last day to certify for a window is its closing date minus 28 days: The FCC Form 470 must be certified in EPC at least 28 days before the close of the FCC Form 471 filing window for the funding year in which services and/or products are requested. (FCC Form 470 Filing). A Form 470 certified that day reaches its allowable contract date on the closing day, and The FCC Form 471 must be certified on or before 11:59 p.m. ET, on the date that the filing window closes. (Funding Year 2026 Filing Window)

After the window: FY2027 deadlines set by rule

FCC Form 486 (still required in FY2027)

FCC Form 486 must be certified no later than 120 days after the service start date shown on the FCC Form 486 or 120 days after the date of the FCDL, whichever date is later. (FCC Form 486 Filing) USAC's FY2026 reminder: On October 29, 2026 is the deadline to file a FY2026 FCC Form 486 if your FCDL is dated on or before July 1, 2026 and your service start date is July 1, 2026. The same count for FY2027 gives Friday, October 29, 2027 when the FCDL is dated on or before July 1, 2027 and service starts that day. Our arithmetic

For FY2028 and later funding years, applicants don't file it: The FCC also removed the requirement that applicants file the FCC Form 486 for future funding years, beginning in FY2028, and directed USAC to transfer the CIPA compliance certifications to the FCC Form 471. The FCC noted that applicants with open commitments or approved appeals from prior funding years will continue to use the FCC Form 486 to certify CIPA compliance. Please be sure to file the FCC Form 486 in FY2026 and FY2027. (E-Rate News Brief (May 2026); the order is FCC 26-30, which USAC's pages link.)

Invoices (BEAR and SPI)

Under the amended rule on USAC's Step 7: Invoicing page, the Invoice Deadline Date (IDD) is no later than the latest of the following: 120 days after the last date to receive service (or service delivery deadline). 120 days after the date of the Funding Commitment Decision Letter. (two more triggers cover revised decisions and rejected invoices). Upon request, invoice filers are entitled to a one-time, 120-day deadline extension per FRN. The request must be made before, or within 15 days after, the original IDD.

USAC's FY2025 example: For example, the invoice filing deadline for recurring services in FY2025 is Wednesday, October 28, 2026. That would extend the invoice filing deadline to Thursday, February 25, 2027, which is 120 days after the original invoice filing deadline of October 28, 2026.

For FY2027 recurring services, 120 days after June 30, 2028 is Saturday, October 28, 2028. So expect USAC to show Monday, October 30, 2028. Service delivery

Recurring services must be delivered during the relevant funding year (July 1 through June 30). In general, non-recurring services must be delivered and installed between July 1 of the relevant funding year and September 30 following the June 30 close of that funding year (i.e., 15 months after the beginning of the funding year). Special construction, which is also a non-recurring service, must be delivered and the network must be lit or in use by June 30 of the applicable funding year. (Service Delivery) For FY2027 that is June 30, 2028 for recurring services and special construction, and September 30, 2028 for other non-recurring services.

SPAC (FCC Form 473), for service providers

Service providers can file a SPAC Form after the FCC Form 471 application filing window opens for the upcoming funding year. Service providers must file their SPAC Form before any FCC Forms 472 (BEAR) or FCC Forms 474 (SPI) invoices can be processed and paid by USAC. the SPAC Form should be filed no later than June 30 of the applicable funding year (i.e., June 30, 2025, for SPINs with FY 2024 FRNs) (File the FCC Form 473) For FY2027 that reads as June 30, 2028. USAC's data for FY2026 is in our free FY2026 SPAC figures, and one SPIN's status is in the E-Rate Provider Check.

What changes after FY2027

From FY2028, bidding moves to a USAC portal and the Form 486 goes away (above). USAC's E-Rate News Brief (May 2026): The order establishes a competitive bidding portal for FY2028 that will require prospective service providers to submit bids in the USAC-managed portal. FY2027 bidding still runs on the FCC Form 470 in EPC.

Quick answers

When is the FY2027 E-Rate Form 471 application filing window?
USAC has not announced it (checked November 6, 2026); USAC says it opens in early 2027. The last three windows ran Jan 17, 2024 to Mar 27, 2024, Jan 15, 2025 to Mar 26, 2025, and Jan 21, 2026 to Apr 1, 2026.
What is the last day to post a Form 470 for FY2027?
It is 28 days before the FY2027 Form 471 window closes, and USAC has not announced the close yet. Recent cutoffs: Feb 22, 2022, Feb 28, 2023, Feb 28, 2024, Feb 26, 2025 and Mar 4, 2026. USAC usually announces the window in mid- to late December; plan on its date, not a projection.
How does the 28-day Form 470 waiting period count?
In calendar days, weekends and holidays included. The day the FCC Form 470 is certified (its Eastern Time date in EPC) is day 1 and the allowable contract date is day 29, so a form certified on the 1st of a 30- or 31-day month reaches it on the 29th. It is the earliest day to select a provider, sign a contract or certify the FCC Form 471, not always the day. USAC: Applicants may set a later bid deadline by including it in their FCC Form 470 narrative or their request for proposals (RFP). This deadline must be at least 28 days after certifying their FCC Form 470 or longer depending on your state or local procurement rules or regulations. FCC rules require that applicants carefully consider all bids received before they start their bid evaluation process, even if the minimum 28-day waiting period has lapsed. (28-Day Waiting Period)
Is the FCC Form 486 still required for FY2027?
Yes. USAC says the FCC removed the Form 486 beginning in FY2028, and: Please be sure to file the FCC Form 486 in FY2026 and FY2027. It is due 120 days after the later of the service start date and the FCDL date: October 29, 2027 for service starting July 1, 2027 with an FCDL dated on or before that day.
When is the FY2027 E-Rate invoice deadline?
For recurring services, 120 days after FY2027 ends on June 30, 2028, which is Saturday, October 28, 2028; each request's own date is in its FCDL, in EPC and in USAC's FRN data. A later FCDL or revised decision can make it later, and USAC grants one 120-day extension if it is asked for before, or within 15 days after, the deadline.

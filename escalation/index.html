Questions

Which index should I use?
The one your contract names. Escalation clauses usually name a specific BLS series, such as Steel mill products (WPU1017), and a base month, often the bid or contract month. If yours doesn't, that's a question for the parties or a lawyer, not for a calculator. The ConsensusDocs 200.1 amendment and many public-agency specs are common sources of clause language.
What does the P next to a number mean?
Preliminary. BLS says Producer Price Index values "are subject to monthly revisions up to four months after original publication." If your contract fixes the value as first published, keep a dated copy (the share link re-runs against BLS's latest values, which can change). If it uses final values, wait four months or recheck.
How is the adjustment worked out?
Percent change = (current index ÷ base index − 1) × 100. With a trigger, either only the part beyond the trigger counts, or the whole change counts once the trigger is passed, as you choose. A cap limits the percent after that. The adjustment is that percent times your dollar amount, rounded to the cent. Every step is printed with the result.
Why does a month say "no value"?
BLS skips some months: it published no October 2025 CPI after the 2025 federal shutdown, and it withholds some PPI values that don't meet its publication standards. We never fill a missing month; pick the month your contract says to use instead.
Where do the numbers come from, exactly?
From the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. The page carries a copy of each series from 2005 onward (built from BLS's published data files), and asks BLS's public data service for the latest three years of the series you pick. When BLS answers, its values win and any revision since our copy is flagged. When it doesn't, the result says so and uses our copy, with its date.

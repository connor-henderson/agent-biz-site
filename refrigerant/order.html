What you get, by email

  1. Every leak rate recomputed with your site's method (annualizing or rolling average, 40 CFR 84.102), the arithmetic shown, and compared with the rate on your record.
  2. Possible misses, each with its citation: additions with no recorded rate, rates over the trigger with no leak inspection, repair or initial verification test within 30 days, follow-up tests later than 10 days, overdue leak inspections, mixed methods, and gaps in the full-charge and service records (84.106(b)–(l); 82.157 for R-22 and other HCFC/CFC systems of 50 lb or more).
  3. Deadlines still open: repairs, tests, inspections, and automatic leak detection for 1,500 lb systems by January 1, 2027 (84.108).
  4. The 125% screen: which appliances, if any, lost 125% or more of their full charge in 2026 and need a report by March 1, 2027 (84.106(j)). For each one, a worksheet of the twelve items in 84.106(m)(4), filled in as far as your records go. We leave the cause, the repair description and the signature to you.
  5. A 2027 checklist and EPA's own steps for getting a HAWK "Leak Reporter" account in CDX, from EPA's January 2026 webinar.

What you send, after paying

Checkout takes you to a form on this page. No attachments and no email: you paste plain text into the form, or load it straight from the free calculator if you kept your log in this browser. For each appliance:

  • Its name, refrigerant, full charge in pounds and how the full charge was determined, appliance type, and installation year.
  • The last date refrigerant was added before 2026, if you have it.
  • Every 2026 addition: date, pounds, and the leak rate on the ticket. Also any leak inspections, repairs, initial and follow-up verification tests, and retrofit/retirement plans, with dates.

Please don't type technicians' names, phone numbers or email addresses. We only need to know whether the ticket shows a technician ("tech: yes").

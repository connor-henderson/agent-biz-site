Refrigerant leak rate calculator

Since January 1, 2026, EPA's leak repair rule covers appliances holding 15 lb or more of an HFC refrigerant such as R-404A, R-448A or R-134a. That brings in HFC walk-in coolers, supermarket rack systems and process chillers from 15 lb up. The older Section 608 leak rule still covers only appliances with 50 lb or more of an ozone-depleting refrigerant such as R-22. What changed in 2026, in four lines

  • Who: owners and operators of appliances with a full charge of 15 lb or more of a refrigerant that contains an HFC, or a substitute with a global warming potential above 53 (40 CFR 84.106(a)). Residential and light-commercial AC (EPA: equipment for cooling individual rooms, single-family homes and small commercial buildings) is exempt. Chillers are not, and EPA's January 2026 fact sheet says certain VRF systems are not either.
  • When to calculate: every time refrigerant is added, except right after an installation or retrofit, or a seasonal variance (84.106(b)).
  • Repair trigger: a leak rate over 20% for commercial refrigeration, 30% for industrial process refrigeration, or 10% for comfort cooling and everything else. Repairs and an initial verification test are due within 30 days (84.106(c)–(e)).
  • New yearly report: any covered appliance that leaked 125% or more of its full charge in 2026 must be reported to EPA by March 1, 2027 (84.106(j)).

The rules this page uses

Text checked against the eCFR on October 29, 2026. Part 84 subpart C has not changed since it took effect on December 10, 2024 (published October 11, 2024); 82.157 was last amended April 10, 2020.

RuleWhat it says
84.102 "Leak rate"Annualizing: (lb added to return to full charge ÷ full charge) ÷ (shorter of days since refrigerant was last added, or 365 ÷ 365) × 100. Rolling average: lb added over the previous 365 days, or since the last successful follow-up verification test if that's shorter, ÷ full charge × 100. One method for all covered appliances at a facility.
84.106(a)Applies from January 1, 2026 to appliances with 15 lb or more of a refrigerant containing an HFC, or a substitute with GWP above 53. Not to appliances using solely an ozone-depleting refrigerant (those stay under Section 608) or to residential and light-commercial AC and heat pumps.
84.106(b)Calculate at every addition, except right after a retrofit or a new installation, or a seasonal variance. For the first calculation after January 1, 2026, use 365 days (annualizing) or only pounds added since January 1, 2026 (rolling average). EPA's preamble says this substitute is for when records of earlier additions are absent (89 FR 82724).
84.106(c)Repair when the rate is over 20% (commercial refrigeration: retail food and cold storage, including restaurants), 30% (industrial process refrigeration, including ice rinks and industrial ice machines), or 10% (comfort cooling, refrigerated transport, other).
84.106(d)–(f)A certified technician finds and repairs the leaks within 30 days of the addition that exceeded the trigger (120 if an industrial process shutdown is needed), with an initial verification test in that window and a follow-up test within 10 days after. Extensions must be requested from EPA within the same window.
84.106(g)After an exceedance, leak inspections: every 3 months for commercial or industrial process refrigeration with 500 lb or more, otherwise yearly, until the rate stays under the trigger for 4 quarters (or 1 year).
84.106(h)A signed retrofit or retirement plan within 30 days if you won't repair, take no action, or repairs fail.
84.106(j), (m)(4)Leaked 125% or more of full charge in a calendar year: report to EPA by March 1 of the next year, electronically. EPA's reporting tool is HAWK, reached through its CDX portal with the "Leak Reporter" role (EPA webinar, Jan. 8, 2026).
84.106(k)Purged refrigerant destroyed at 98% efficiency or better doesn't count toward the leak rate.
84.108Commercial or industrial process refrigeration with 1,500 lb or more: automatic leak detection on installation or within 30 days of it (installed from 2026), or by January 1, 2027 for systems installed 2017–2025.
82.157 (Section 608)Appliances with 50 lb or more of a CFC or HCFC refrigerant (such as R-22), or a blend containing one: the same formulas, triggers, repair deadlines and 125% report. Extension requests and chronic-leak reports go to 608reports@epa.gov (82.157(m)). No 2026 transition rule: you need the actual date of the previous addition.

Pending, not yet law

  • EPA proposed on May 26, 2026 to exempt road and intermodal container transport refrigeration units from Part 84 leak repair (FR Doc. 2026-10388; comments closed July 10, 2026). As of November 1, 2026 no final rule has been published.
  • Grocery groups have asked EPA to reconsider the leak repair rule (for example FMI's petition). EPA's May 2026 final rule on related HFC rules (FR Doc. 2026-10387) changed technology-transition dates, not the leak repair section.

How this page reads the gray areas

  • Several additions marked "same leak" are added together (as 84.102 Step 1 allows), and the days are counted from the addition before that leak to its first top-up. Two additions on the same day are combined. This gives the higher of the two possible rates.
  • Additions before January 1, 2026 (Part 84): the rule text says to use 365 days, or pounds since January 1, 2026, for the first calculation. EPA's preamble says that is for when records of earlier additions are absent. So if you enter a 2025 addition, this page uses it, which can only raise the rate. Leave 2025 out only if you have no record of it.
  • An appliance with an HCFC/HFC blend (such as R-408A) at 50 lb or more falls under both rules. This page then calculates under Section 608, which has no 2026 transition, so earlier additions you enter always count.
  • Deadlines shown are the basic ones. They don't account for mothballing (which pauses the clock), approved extensions, or automatic leak detection replacing inspections.
  • Variable refrigerant flow (VRF): the 2024 rule preamble put VRF in the exempt residential and light-commercial group, but EPA's January 2026 fact sheet says certain VRF systems are in their own subsector and not in that group. Ask EPA before treating a large VRF system as exempt.
  • Additions with no calculation required (seasonal variance, right after installation or retrofit) are left out of later rolling sums and the 125% yearly total, because they don't replace leaked refrigerant. Their dates still count as the last day refrigerant was added.
  • "Over" the trigger means strictly greater: exactly 20.0% does not trigger the 20% rule.
  • Refrigerant makeup (which blends contain an HFC or an HCFC) follows the ASHRAE Standard 34 designations. Check your cylinder or SDS. GWPs for non-HFC refrigerants are from 84.64(b).
  • Which "appliance type" fits is your call, using the definitions in 84.102. EPA's January 2026 fact sheet summarizes the rule.

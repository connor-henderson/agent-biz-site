Record-keeping checklist for 2027

  • Keep every record for at least three years (40 CFR 84.106(l); 82.157(l)). Appliance records (full charge, how it was determined, revisions, installation date) are kept until three years after the appliance is retired (84.106(l)(1)).
  • One method, annualizing or rolling average, for every covered appliance at the site (84.102 "Leak rate"). Write down which one.
  • At every addition: the leak rate and method on the service record, with the date, parts serviced, type of service, technician's name, and pounds and type of refrigerant (84.106(b), (l)(2), (l)(4)). The free calculator does the arithmetic: https://westworkshq.com/refrigerant/
  • Over the trigger (20% commercial refrigeration, 30% industrial process, 10% comfort cooling and other; 84.106(c)(2)): leak inspection and repair with a passed initial verification test within 30 days (120 with an industrial process shutdown), then a follow-up test within 10 days (84.106(d)–(e)). Keep the inspection and test records (84.106(l)(5), (l)(7)).
  • Can't make 30 days for a qualifying reason? Request the extension from EPA within the same 30 days (84.106(f)(4)). Won't repair? A signed retrofit or retirement plan within 30 days (84.106(h)).
  • After a repair, leak inspections every three months (500 lb or more, commercial or industrial process) or yearly, from the follow-up test, until the appliance stays under the trigger (84.106(g)(1)).
  • Commercial or industrial process refrigeration with 1,500 lb or more installed 2017–2025: automatic leak detection by January 1, 2027 (84.108(b)(2)).
  • At year end, total each appliance's additions. 125% or more of full charge in a calendar year means a report by March 1 of the next year (84.106(j)). The first Part 84 reports, for 2026, are due March 1, 2027.

Getting into HAWK before March 1 (from EPA's Jan. 8, 2026 webinar)

  • Go to https://cdx.epa.gov/ and register (or log in). New users create a CDX user ID and sign in through Login.gov.
  • Add the program service "HFC-ODS-R" (Hydrofluorocarbon and Ozone Depleting Substances Reporting) and pick the role "Leak Reporter" (or "Company Representative").
  • Find your organization (or add it), enter the validation code CDX emails you from helpdesk@epacdx.net, complete the LexisNexis identity check and sign the Electronic Signature Agreement. A Leak Reporter can use HAWK once the agreement is signed.
  • To report: log into CDX, click the "Leak Reporter" role link to open HAWK, choose Leak Reporting, then "Submit Report" under HFC Leak Report (or ODS Leak Report), and sign and submit.
  • Section 608 (R-22 and other HCFC/CFC appliances): reports go by email to 608reports@epa.gov (82.157(m)); EPA's webinar says HAWK also accepts them.

Our readings, and what this check is not

  • Additions with no calculation required (seasonal variance, right after installation or retrofit) are left out of later rolling sums and the 125% total, because they replace no leaked refrigerant. The rule text doesn't say this outright; if EPA reads it literally, the totals above would be higher. These rows are listed separately in each table.
  • Several additions marked "same leak" are summed, with days counted from the addition before that leak (the higher of the possible rates). Two additions on the same day are combined.
  • Under Part 84, an addition before January 1, 2026 gets no calculation of its own, but if you sent it, it counts as the earlier addition for 2026 (EPA's preamble, 89 FR 82724, allows ignoring earlier additions only when records are absent).
  • We don't model mothballing beyond flagging it, approved extensions beyond noting them, or automatic leak detection replacing inspections.
  • We only see what you sent. We did not see your appliances, your technicians' tickets or anything at EPA. We don't file anything with EPA, sign anything, or contact EPA for you.

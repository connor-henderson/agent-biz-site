Method, limits and sources

Limits, in plain words.

  • Snow varies over short distances. The range column shows the lowest and highest observer within 10 miles; your lot can sit anywhere in it, and occasionally outside it.
  • The median often runs below the airport figure you see in the news. Both are shown: the median is what nearby observers measured, and the airport figure is the one NWS climate reports quote.
  • Trigger counts near a threshold can move. A storm at 1.0 in can become 0.9 in when NOAA adds or revises an observer report, which moves it across your 1-inch trigger. Check the range before you bill on a borderline storm.
  • Times come from the airport station, which can be several miles away. Rows marked station-only have dates but no times (observers measured snow, the airport reported none).
  • Preliminary rows change. NOAA posts observer reports 3 to 4 days late and revises its snowfall grid for 5 days. A preliminary row is followed by a final one.
  • Refreeze is airport air temperature, not your pavement temperature.

How the ledger is built.

  • An event starts at the first and ends at the last hourly or special airport observation reporting falling snow, sleet, snow pellets, freezing rain or freezing drizzle; a gap of more than 6 hours starts a new event. Blowing or drifting snow alone does not count. One or two flurry observations with nothing measured are dropped. So are reports stuck on snow. Such a run repeats the same moderate or heavy snow, sleet or snow pellets (no freezing rain or drizzle) in 3 or more hourly reports in a row, each with a precipitation reading under 0.01 in. It is dropped only when one of its reports is contradicted. Its own temperature can do that (a freezing code, such as freezing fog, at 35F or more). So can the 7 a.m. observers within 10 miles, if 3 or more reported and all measured 0.0 for the observation day the report falls in.
  • A station-only event is a day when 2 or more observers within 10 miles measured 0.1 in or more but the airport reported no frozen precipitation. It has dates but no start and end times.
  • Ledger snowfall is the median event total of all GHCN-Daily stations within 10 miles (3 or more stations); otherwise the nearest station; otherwise the NOHRSC grid cell. With an even number of stations the median is the mean of the two middle totals; every figure is rounded to the nearest 0.1 in, halves up (4.35 becomes 4.4). A station counts only if it reported every observation day of the event (stations that skipped a day are listed in the source rows but not counted). Denver Central Park 1 SSE (USC00052221) reports Denver Central Park's snowfall, so the two count once, as Denver Central Park. In a snowy season (median station total 5 in or more), observers who logged no snowfall all season are dropped as rain-only reporters. Each station-day goes to the one event its observation window overlaps most. NWS stations (IDs starting USW) are read as midnight to midnight local standard time. Denver Central Park (USW00023062), a cooperative station, is read as the 24 hours ending 7 a.m. MST through January 2026. Other observers are assumed to measure the 24 hours ending 7 a.m. (the CoCoRaHS standard; cooperative observers' times vary). A station-only event never takes an NWS station's midnight-to-midnight day on which an event's airport reports fall: that day goes to the one of those events it overlaps most.
  • NWS airport is the event total at the nearest NWS station with an ID starting USW (within 25 miles) that reported snowfall for every day of the event, never Denver Central Park or the Blue Hill Observatory near Boston (cooperative stations, not airports). Here that is the station behind NWS daily climate reports and most news coverage. It is shown for comparison; it is not your site's snowfall. No figure is shown when a snowy day at that station is shared with another listed event (unless that event measured nothing): one day's total can't be split.
  • NOHRSC grid is the sum of the 24-hour analyses (ending 6 a.m. CST) whose windows overlap the event, read at the site's 0.04-degree grid cell (about 2.7 by 2 miles).
  • Ice is flagged when the airport reported freezing rain or drizzle, its ice-accretion sensor recorded ice, or a storm report of freezing rain or ice was filed within 10 miles.
  • Refreeze is the first time within 72 hours after the event that the airport air temperature fell to 31F or below after rising to 33F or above. It is air temperature at the airport, not pavement temperature at your site.
  • Certified copies. Each event names the nearest GHCN-Daily station and dates. NOAA NCEI sells Department of Commerce certified copies of those records for court use (https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/services/certification).

How often snowfall reached 1, 2, 3 and 6 inches at 8 metro airports, winters 2021-22 to 2025-26

Over the last five complete winters (October 1 to April 30, 2021-22 through 2025-26), the main airports of these eight metros averaged from 3.6 days a winter with 2 inches of snow or more (Boston) to 13.2 (Buffalo). The count moved a lot from one winter to the next: Minneapolis-St Paul had 16 such days in 2022-23 and 3 in 2023-24. Days of 6 inches or more averaged 0.2 (Chicago) to 3.4 (Buffalo) a winter. Each count is of climate days (midnight to midnight, local standard time) in NOAA's daily snowfall record for the airport, so a storm that crosses midnight is split between two days.

Days, not storms

A storm that crosses midnight is split between two climate days. MSP airport recorded 8.9 inches in the March 14–15, 2026 storm: 5.0 on one climate day and 3.9 on the next, so the first table counts it as two days of 3 inches or more and no day of 6 inches. Adding up consecutive days of snow would put that storm back together, but daily totals cannot show where one storm ends and the next begins: Chicago had measurable snow on 9 straight days (January 5–13, 2024), 4 of them with 2 inches or more, and Buffalo on 11 straight days (January 31 – February 10, 2025), 7.2 inches in all but never more than 1.7 in a day. So this page counts days, not storms.

Reading the counts against a snow contract

  • Seasonal or per-occurrence. A seasonal price is set for the whole winter before it starts; a per-push, per-event or per-inch price is paid for each occurrence. So unless a seasonal contract caps the pushes or inches, the spread between winters is weather risk its price carries: at Minneapolis-St Paul (MSP), days of 2 inches or more ran from 3 in 2023-24 to 16 in 2022-23.
  • Each contract sets its own trigger and its own measuring stick. A snow removal request for quotes on the State of Iowa's bid site makes the contractor responsible for snow removal “when snow accumulation of more than two (2) inches occurs”, tells it to expect the work when “the snow accumulates two inches or more (as measured by the closest National Weather Service or local airport)”, and says that for invoice audits “snow totals will be based on reports from the National Weather Service https://www.weather.gov/ and/or those reported by a local news channel/source” (RFQ MS245820005, Attachment 1). The State of Connecticut's terms for snow removal at a Department of Motor Vehicles office have the contractor respond “whenever a storm begins that is expected to deposit any amount of snow” and price per event: an event “may last more than twenty four (24) hours and require multiple return visits”, and “Per Event rates as specified on Exhibit B remain in effect regardless of how many visits the Contractor makes to the Facility during the Event” (contract 19PSX0230, Exhibit A).
  • Days, storms and visits are different counts. One storm can fall on two climate days (above), and one storm can need several visits. Wording matters at the edge too: 24 of the 270 days of 2 inches or more in the first table were exactly 2.0 inches, which “two inches or more” counts and “more than two (2) inches” does not. The Iowa document above uses both.
  • The airport is not your site. In the March 14–15, 2026 storm, the 37 observers in NOAA's daily record within 10 miles of Minneapolis City Hall that reported every day of it measured from 3.7 to 11.9 inches, while MSP airport recorded 8.9. Those figures come from the public sample of Snow Ledger, our per-ZIP storm-by-storm ledger for your sites. The Connecticut contract above also allows that “one Facility may receive higher accumulation totals due to the topography of the location of the Facility”.

Method

  • Data. Daily snowfall (element SNOW, in inches to tenths) from NOAA NCEI's Global Historical Climatology Network – Daily (GHCN-Daily), one request per airport to NCEI's data service for October 1, 2021 to April 30, 2026, retrieved November 11, 2026: MSP · ORD · MKE · DTW · DEN · BUF · CLE · BOS.
  • Stations. Each metro's main airport, which is also the station of the National Weather Service's F-6 monthly climate report for the city (the F-6 station name is in parentheses):

  • Climate day. NWS climate sites report each day from midnight to midnight local standard time (NWS Instruction 10-1004, sections 4.1.2 and 4.3.3), so while daylight saving time is on, a day runs from 1 a.m. to 1 a.m. by the clock. NWS daily snowfall includes ice pellets and hail (the same instruction, its key to column 8 of the F-6 report).
  • Thresholds. A day counts when its snowfall is at or above 1.0, 2.0, 3.0 or 6.0 inches. A trace (T) is a reported day below every threshold.
  • Missing days. A day of the winter with no value from NCEI, or a value NCEI's quality checks flagged, is missing: it is shown in the Missing days column and never counted as zero. A winter with more than 5 missing days is flagged: its counts are shown as reported and it is left out of the average. In these data NCEI has a value for every day of all 40 airport-winters (8,488 days) and none failed its quality checks, so no winter is flagged.
  • Averages are the mean of the winters that are not flagged (here all five), rounded half up to one decimal. Oct–Apr snow is the sum of the daily values from October 1 to April 30, not the snowfall “since Jul 1” that NWS daily climate reports total (the same instruction, its CLI format).
  • Cross-check. We compared every day with the NWS F-6 monthly climate report for the same airport and month (the newest complete issuance in the two days after the month ended), from the Iowa Environmental Mesonet's archive of NWS text products. They match on 8,449 of 8,488 days. Of the rest, 23 are a trace in one and 0.0 in the other; on 15 the F-6 marks snowfall missing where NCEI's record has 0.0 (listed below the table); and 1 differs in amount (Buffalo, January 10, 2025: 1.3 inches in NCEI's record, 1.0 in the F-6). Counting with the F-6 values instead changes no counts in the first table. NCEI's source flags show where its values come from: its ASOS summary of the day (flag W) on 6,626 days, which match the F-6 on 6,594; the F-6 reports themselves (flag 1) on 1,859 days from January 1, 2025 on, which match the F-6 on 1,853; and additions or replacements made through NCEI's Datzilla process (flag Z) on 3 days, which match the F-6 on 2. NCEI's record and the F-6 are two records of the same airport stations, so this checks the record-keeping, not the measuring; and on the 1,859 days with flag 1, NCEI's value came from the F-6 itself, so there it checks only the copy.

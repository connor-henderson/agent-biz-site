What's in a brief

  • Key dates: the question deadline; any site visit, and whether it's mandatory; the proposal due date and time zone; the opening and award dates. USAC's data has only the earliest contract date. The due date is in the RFP, and it can fall weeks later.
  • How to bid: the portal and its registration; how many copies, in what file formats; who you may contact, and how.
  • What they want: items and quantities by site, brands and "or equivalent" terms, licensing terms, and installation.
  • How they score: every criterion and its points.
  • What the RFP says is required: what it calls mandatory, its stated disqualifiers, the forms it lists, insurance minimums and the pricing format. It isn't a complete compliance checklist: the RFP's own list governs.
  • Watch-outs: places where the applicant's documents disagree; files the RFP refers to that weren't uploaded to USAC; Form 470 lines the RFP leaves out.
  • The account, from USAC's public data: the current vendor and how many bids it drew, when the contract ends, the discount rate and the Category 2 budget left.

Who it's for, and when it's worth $29

A brief saves the most reading on long or multi-file RFPs. Of the 88 open Texas FY2027 Form 470s with PDF RFP files on USAC, the median has 6 pages; 41 have 5 or fewer and 17 have 21 or more (USAC's files, measured 2026-11-04). On a short RFP the dates are usually on page 1, and you may not need us.

How we check it

Before a brief goes out, code checks that every date, time, number and quote in it matches the cited page of the applicant's file or USAC's data, and that each weekday matches its date. A keyword sweep for disqualifiers, mandatory events and rejection terms is logged hit by hit; each hit is either in the brief or marked not applicable. Code can't check judgment calls or image-only pages, so those are marked.

What a brief can't see. It's built from the files on USAC's Form 470 record on the date it shows. Applicants often post addenda, Q&A and some attachments only on their own bid portals, and those aren't in it. Pages that are only images are read and marked, or listed as unread. Check the official RFP and every addendum before you rely on a line.

What's installed now? A brief doesn't list the applicant's current equipment. The free What's Installed Now lookup shows the equipment on the applicant's funded E-Rate requests, from USAC's records.

What goes in the proposal

The binder format is from Doc A p. 4. The online form fields are from Doc B pp. 3–4 and 17–19.

  • Binder: 3-ring binders, with tabs for four sections:
    1. Insurance certificate and cover letter.
    2. Executive summary: technical summary, value added, and an itemized cost breakdown by campus in Excel. The columns are campus, part number, quantity, unit price and total. "Failure to include an Excel formatted document will result in the deduction of points."
    3. A complete copy of the RFP with its sections filled in. "Incomplete documents may be awarded 0 points".
    4. Exceptions to the RFP or to the base contract (Appendix A).
  • Required uploads in the online system: cover letter, the three executive-summary items, exceptions, certificate of liability, Texas Form 1295, and the district's vendor packet.
  • Online answers in the bid system (Doc B pp. 17–19):
    • Agreement to E-Rate payment rules, and to invoice USAC directly via SPI.
    • Company qualifications: SPIN, years in business, years of E-Rate work, staff count, and the project manager.
    • 3 references. At least 2 must involve E-Rate purchases or services, and each reference should expect a 15–20-minute call.
    • An acknowledgement.
  • Before contract signing: a SPIN and a Service Provider Annual Certification (SPAC) (Doc A p. 2).
  • Vendor status: an authorized dealer, distributor or manufacturer for the products bid (Doc B p. 5).
  • Insurance minimums (Doc B p. 16):
    • General liability: $1M per occurrence and $2M aggregate, with Bryan ISD as additional insured.
    • Workers' comp: statutory, plus employer's liability of $1M.
    • Auto: $1M.
    • Professional (E&O): $1M per claim and $2M aggregate.
  • Pricing terms:
    • Quote "a quantity one price" (Doc B p. 6).
    • FOB destination, with no freight charges (Doc B pp. 5, 8).
    • Prices must hold for 60 days after the due date (Doc A p. 7).
    • Net 30 payment (Doc B p. 12).
  • Also in the terms (Doc B pp. 13 and 17): Texas prevailing-wage compliance, worker criminal-history limits on campuses, and a Texas-resident preference that may apply in a tie. DIR contract holders may say so (Doc A p. 3).

Watch-outs: the two documents disagree

  • How to submit:
    • Doc A asks for four sealed copies (1 original, 1 hard copy, 2 digital) at the Purchasing Office, and says faxed or emailed proposals aren't accepted (Doc A p. 3).
    • Doc B accepts proposals "submitted online or sealed and delivered" and "encourages online submission" (Doc B p. 9).
  • Where to send questions:
    • Doc A says in writing by email or fax to the district contact it names (Doc A pp. 3, 10).
    • Doc B says "All questions must be submitted on the 'Questions' tab" of the online bid (Doc B p. 4).
  • The tie-break rule: both documents say the 'more stringent requirement' governs where they conflict (Doc A p. 4; Doc B p. 5).
  • Contact rules: contact with board members or staff during the process "may result in disqualification" (Doc B p. 9).
  • Late proposals: "Any responses received after the submittal deadline date and time will be disqualified" (Doc B p. 9), and late proposals "will be returned unopened" (Doc B p. 7).
  • Copyright marks: the bid system's terms say "Copyrighted are unacceptable and may be disqualified" (Doc B p. 9).
  • Files missing from USAC's copy: Doc B lists four attachments in the bid system (Doc B p. 2):
    • the hardware list;
    • a draft contract;
    • a federal vendor packet;
    • prevailing wage rates.

USAC's copy has the hardware list (Attachment 1, Doc A pp. 10–12) but not the draft contract, the vendor packet or the wage rates. Those are in the district's bid system.

  • Where addenda appear: on the district's E-Rate page and website, and in its bid system (Doc A p. 3). Doc A doesn't name USAC as a place they're posted.

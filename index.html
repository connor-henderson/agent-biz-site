Why this exists

Across 855 laundromat sales on BizBuySell from 2021 to 2025, the middle half closed between 2.72× and 4.50× seller's discretionary earnings (SDE), with a median of 3.50×. So a price only means something if the earnings are real. In our sample report (a hypothetical listing, built to show the method), the listing claims $133,000 of SDE on $310,000 of revenue. After rent (reading the listing's rent figure as monthly), that leaves about $92,000 a year for utilities, two employees, repairs, and insurance, and only about $31,000 if utilities run at the industry-survey average. Two full-time employees at the Texas minimum wage cost $30,160 of that. The teardown runs that math on your listing, shows what earnings the asking price assumes, checks whether an SBA loan would clear its coverage minimum, and lists what the seller still has to prove.

Sources: BizBuySell laundromat valuation benchmarks (sold listings, 2021–2025), as reproduced by Iconic, July 2026; American Coin-Op 2026 operator survey (utilities averaged 19.5% of revenue in 2025). Full citations are in every report.

What's in the report

  • Earnings quality: what's left after rent and typical utilities to pay staff and upkeep, what earnings the asking price assumes, and which records will confirm or sink the claim.
  • The claimed numbers, like for like: price ÷ SDE against sold-deal multiples, plus EBITDA-like and revenue cross-checks.
  • Capacity and water-bill checks: how hard the machines must work to produce the claimed revenue, the wash cycles that revenue implies, and how to check them against the water bills.
  • Financing: the down payment and payments on an SBA loan at current maximum rates, whether the earnings clear SBA's 1.25× coverage minimum, and how much that leaves for your own pay.
  • Equipment: age against industry life ranges, and a rough replacement cost, clearly labeled as an estimate.
  • Lease check: years left against a 10-year SBA loan, what to require from the landlord, and rent as a share of revenue.
  • Area: U.S. Census figures for the store's county, or for its census tract if you share the address.
  • A checklist of 14 questions for the broker and 5 for your lender, filled in with this listing's own figures and gaps.
